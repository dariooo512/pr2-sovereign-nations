# Port Royale 2: Sovereign Nations — features

Found your own nation in the Caribbean as a fifth power next to England, France, Holland and Spain, and keep it alive.
Full rules: `PLAYER_GUIDE.md`. What changed in each version: `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Found a nation.** With enough gold and ships, declare the independence of a town where you own property. It becomes
your capital and leaves its old power. On one founding card, choose one of eight forms of state, type your own name
(or keep the suggestion) and pick one of 100 flags with the game's logo selector. The flag then flies on
the nation's towns and fleets; your own convoys keep your own flag (the logo you chose for the new game).

**The nation panel.** The flag button in the top bar opens a window in the style of the game's Log: six tabs for the
Overview, Treasury, Towns, Diplomacy, Government, and Navy and defence. Each tab is a copy of the game window that
does the same job: the Treasury is the *Balance* page, Towns the Chronicle's *Town list*, Diplomacy its *Nations*
relations, the Navy the *Convoy list*; click a row to select a town, a power or a fleet.

**Money.** A national treasury separate from your own gold. Towns pay taxes (a national rate or a rate per town),
imports pay a tariff, towns, fleets and towers cost upkeep; the accounts are settled every month and kept
in 13 categories, last month and a forecast, like the game's own balance.

**Territory.** Gain towns by conquest in a war, by buying them from their power, or when they petition to join. Lose
them to an enemy's war fleet, or when a town in long unrest secedes. The capital moves when it falls. In your own
towns the treasurer, commander, governor and building master serve you as their sovereign, whatever your reputation
with the town's former power. You hold the
building permit in every town of the nation; a town that leaves goes back to the permit you had there before.

**Diplomacy.** War, ceasefire, peace, trade agreements, non-aggression pacts and alliances, with the chance shown for
every proposal. The powers recognize you over time, make offers, and declare war on their own, depending on their
strategy (commercial, expansionist, defensive or opportunistic) — but never without a warning: a power that considers
war says so a month ahead and why, and the Diplomacy tab shows each relation's target and its causes.

**Government.** Five forms — Sovereign, Merchant Republic, Colonial Monarchy, Pirate Republic, Maritime Federation —
each with strengths and weaknesses; a reform costs stability and is possible every 25 months.

**Stability and loyalty.** The nation's stability and each town's loyalty respond to taxes, wars, solvency and
conquest. Low loyalty leads to strikes and, after four months, to secession. The game's town info window of a nation
town shows *Your nation* with the nation's flag and the town's loyalty, kept up to date while the window is open.

**National navy.** Fleets sailed by the game's own military AI: they patrol, raid enemy trade, besiege enemy towns, and
defend yours; they never attack a power you are at peace with (a ceasefire ends their raids). Your naval rank grows with the nation and unlocks more fleets and orders; each fleet can also get its own
order. Recruit soldiers and build cannon towers to defend your towns. The fleets show on the sea map and the
minimap under the nation's flag, light up the sea around them like your own convoys, and the eye on the Navy tab centres
the map on that ship (Ctrl+Shift+N cycles through them).

**Saves.** Everything is part of the normal save game; saves without a nation load as usual.
